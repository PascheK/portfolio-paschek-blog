## 1. Where I left off

In Devlog #0, I said I wanted to make a small 3D game starring my hamster, Bouboulle. One obvious thing was missing: the hamster. This episode is about how I modelled it, and mostly about how I got it wrong before getting it right.

## 2. First attempt: the simple method

I started with a simple technique in **Blender**: I block out the general shape of the hamster, **extrude** it, then apply a **mirror** so everything is symmetrical. After that, I add details all around.

<Video src="/blogs/devlog-1/first-try-blender.mp4" label="First attempt in Blender: the shape of the hamster, then the extrusion" width="960" height="540" />
<Caption>First attempt in Blender: the shape of the hamster, then the extrusion.</Caption>

But when I looked at it in **Unreal Engine 5**, I didn't like the result: the model felt **too small** and **not very well made**. Rather than keep working on it, I decided to start over.

<Video src="/blogs/devlog-1/first-try-unreal.mp4" label="The first model in Unreal Engine 5" width="960" height="540" />
<Caption>The first model in Unreal Engine 5.</Caption>

## 3. Starting over, with references

This time, before reopening Blender, I looked for **references**. That's where I came across **chibis**, those characters with a big head and a small body. I liked the style, so I rebuilt my hamster as a chibi.

## 4. A naked hamster

One thing still bothered me: my hamster was **completely naked**.

## 5. What I take from it

- I didn't like my first model, and that's fine. It left me with a rule: when I want to create something, I **start by looking for references**.
- The model is far from perfect. But I can finally **build something**, and for a game that only existed as an idea, that's a real change.
